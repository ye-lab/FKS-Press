CULTURE DAY

By Absalat Minale

Culture day is one representation of the diversity and unity of students in our school. This tradition started in 2024 by the current Aurelian Seniors and continued to grow as an important celebration of our diversity. The 2025 culture day was the second ever in FKS High School organized by juniors.

The grade 11 students celebrated culture day on Monday December 08, 2025. At the beginning of the event, students made an entrance through the red carpet, each showing the traditional dance of the culture they represented. They continued with other programs including drama, poem, dance, and choreography. They also brought a live chicken to the show, surprising everyone. Despite being organized by junior students, every participant had the opportunity to celebrate and enjoy their time.

I think the whole program was worth it. We did put a lot of effort starting from the red carpet to actually bringing a chicken. I personally think these tiny details were the ones which gave beauty to the program and were worth the effort. — Dagmawit Mesfin (Grade 11)
The hardest part of organizing the whole program was managing time and coordinating everyone... even though it was stressful, this experience taught us teamwork, unity, and responsibility. — Edom Tesfaneh (Grade 11)

Robel Dawit (Grade 12): The event was an amazing one. Lots of colors and cultures representing the diverse cultures of Ethiopia. The intro was the one that impressed me the most.

Eldana Deresse (Grade 9): The entrance, entrance song, drama, and choreography were good and represented the cultures well. However, the speaker wasn't working properly.

Amen Abreham (Grade 12): It was a well-coordinated program from beginning to end. Different varieties of programs ranging from entertainment to education.

In conclusion, 2025 culture day was a great way of showing our diverse culture and unity. While there were a few challenges it was a memorable day and we can't wait for the next event.

Fountain of Knowledge School High School Question and Answer Competition

By Soliyana Seifu

Academic competitions are organized with the intent of boosting learning, critical thinking, and public speaking in a competitive environment. They help improve students' focus and retention while allowing teachers to assess performance and identify learning gaps. With this aim, Fountain of Knowledge School held a Question and Answer competition for selected high school students. The competition involved Mathematics and English and was held from late December to early January.

From Grade 9, Eldana Getachew and Bereket Messay from Section A; Yoseph Dereje and Sarem Belachew from Section B; Mariamawit Mekari and Eliana Yalemsew from Section C; and Bilen Girum and Abel Efrem from Section D participated. Eldana Getachew from 9A, Sarem Belachew from 9B, and Bereket Messay from 9A secured first, second, and third place respectively. Section-wise, 9A ranked first, followed by 9B, 9D, and 9C.

From Grade 10, Yanet Sisay and Bereket Ashenafi from Section A; Fenet Girma and Lielina Seifu from Section B; Liya Mohammed and Fitsum Asnake from Section C; and Alazar Agazi and Tihtina Desta from Section D participated. Fitsum Asmake from 10C placed first, Fenet Girma from 10B second, and Bereket Ashenafi from 10A third. Section-wise, 10B ranked first, followed by 10A, 10C, and 10D.

From Grade 11, participants included Tinsae Getachew, Dagimawit Mesfin, and Alazar Masresha from Section A; Abigail Mesfin, Yebezawit Alemayehu, and Eyoel Getachew from Section B; and Saron Leulseged and Yohannes Afework from Section C. Yebezawit Alemayehu from 11B secured first place, followed by Eyoel Getachew from 11B in second place and Tinsae Getachew from 11A in third. Section-wise, 11B ranked first, followed by 11A and 11C.

However, some students expressed concern that the competition was not open to all interested participants and suggested that future events allow broader participation.

In conclusion, the Question and Answer competition was a wholesome and engaging academic event that helped students test their knowledge and learn from their mistakes in an enjoyable way. Participants noted that the questions were not overly difficult but required careful thinking and concentration.

How to Find Your Future Career

By Dagmawit Mesfin

We all make choices, but in the end our choices make us. — Ken Levine

A career choice is one of the most important decisions you make in your life. Roughly 4 out of 10 students worldwide are unclear about their career expectations, a number that has doubled in the last decade.

Steps:

  1. Self-assessment: Identify your interests and skills. It could be any subject or activity that excites you.
  2. Research: Explore what you need to do to get there. Learn about industries and gain insights.
  3. Gain experience: Volunteer for occasions or clubs related to that profession, attend events, and stay active.
  4. Create a plan: Start targeting colleges and industries and remain flexible. Look for people who have achieved your dream role and learn from them.
  5. Self-assurance: Trust yourself in your choice, but always have a backup plan.

When is the right time to choose your career?
There is no single right time to choose a career. It is an ongoing process that often starts in your teenage years and continues as you gain experience.

Choosing a career should be a personal decision, but family, friends, and society often influence choices.

A career built with passion provides job satisfaction, purpose, well-being, and motivation to improve. Passion should be prioritized over other aspects.

The only way to do great work is to love what you do. If you haven't found it yet, keep looking. Don't settle. — Steve Jobs

Is School Preparing Us for Real Life?

By Hasset Abiye

School is meant to prepare us for the future, but often it focuses more on exams than real-life skills. While it teaches some important basics, it doesn’t always emphasize the skills we need outside the classroom.

On the positive side, school helps develop discipline: meeting deadlines, punctuality, and schedules. Subjects like math and language provide foundational knowledge useful in everyday life. These skills give students a strong starting point for future challenges.

Much of the focus is on grades and memorization. Many students study primarily to pass tests, and what they learn is quickly forgotten. In real life, success depends more on problem-solving, critical thinking, and adaptability.

Another area to strengthen is practical life skills. Many students graduate without guidance on managing money, handling stress, communicating effectively, or making informed career choices.

Schools are increasingly aware of mental health. While academic demands can be stressful, supporting student well-being is a growing priority.

In my view, school provides an important foundation but doesn’t fully prepare students for life beyond the classroom. Balancing academic learning with real-life skills, critical thinking, and well-being would better equip students for the future.
